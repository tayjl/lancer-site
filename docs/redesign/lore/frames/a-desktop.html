Halvard Station

A tidal-locked refuelling hub at the edge of the Merrow shoals, run by habit and the Tidewright Compact.

By vanta9 Nov 20262 min read

Halvard Station sits on a shelf of tidal-locked rock at the edge of the Merrow shoals, and for most of a century it has been the last honest stop before the long crossing.

It began as a refuelling depot. It is now a town in all but name: about four thousand permanent residents, three times that on a busy tide, and a dockside market that never fully closes. The Union survey office lists it as minor infrastructure. Anyone who has argued with the harbourmaster will tell you that is a polite fiction.

The station is built in three rings. The Mooring holds the docks, the cranes and the long market. The Spindle holds housing and administration. The Cistern holds the water-reclamation plant, which hums behind a door that is always, officially, being repaired.

Before you dock

  • Every hull pays a berth tithe to the Tidewright Compact. The rate is posted at the outer buoy and does not move for argument.
  • The lower docks flood on the high tide, so keep your tether clipped.
  • Cassia Vell logs every arrival by hand. Be polite to her pen.
  • Do not use the old signal tower. Nobody will tell you why, and since the Merrow Blackout nobody has tried to.

Posted at the outer buoy, repeated hourly: “Halvard Station welcomes all hulls. Berth tithe applies. The tide does not negotiate.”
